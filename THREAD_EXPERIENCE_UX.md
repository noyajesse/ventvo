# Thread Experience UX

## Purpose

A Thread is a story without a storyteller: a recognizable path through multiple experiences that meaningfully connect. It is discovered from the experience network, not created as a user-managed group or collection.

## Presentation principles

- Lead with the connected experiences. Voice marks are supporting context, not the headline.
- Do not make internal connection records or connection counts the primary content.
- Avoid technical connection-type labels such as `RESPONSE`, `SIMILAR EXPERIENCE`, and `DISCOVERED CONNECTION` in the main experience.
- Do not imply that a Thread is a chat, community, or group of people to join.
- Do not manufacture a thematic title or summary when the available experiences do not support it.
- Keep the originating experience identifiable and provide a path back to it.
- Keep Echo notes attached to their source experience; do not flatten them into separate Thread steps.

## Thread card

A card should communicate:

1. **Story in progress** — quiet framing that this is a discovered path.
2. **Experience preview** — a short excerpt from a real experience in the Thread. This is a preview, not an invented title.
3. **Connected experience count** — supporting context, not a popularity signal.
4. **Additional excerpts** — a small number of real experiences that suggest the path without overwhelming the card.
5. **Explore story** — opens the Thread detail.

Voice marks may appear quietly as context, but must not displace the experiences.

## Thread detail

The detail view should read as a path through experiences:

- Present each experience as a distinct step in a gently connected sequence.
- Show its Voice mark as attribution context and distinguish a Moment from an Echo.
- Keep connection descriptions human-readable and secondary to the experiences themselves.
- Preserve access to the originating experience and its surrounding Space when available.
- Offer a route to explore the wider Map without suggesting that users can join or manage the Thread.

Suggested neutral bridge language:

- “Connected through a shared experience”
- “Connected through a different perspective”
- “Connected through a response”

These descriptions must only be shown when supported by the actual connection type. The Thread itself should remain understandable without reading them.

## Empty and unavailable states

When no Thread exists:

> No stories have taken shape yet.
>
> When enough experiences remain meaningfully connected, a Thread may begin to emerge.

When its experiences are unavailable:

> The connected experiences are no longer available here.

Do not replace unavailable content with generated summaries or inferred personal details.

## Actions and language to avoid

Do not use Join Thread, Follow Thread, Like Thread, Subscribe, Post to Thread, or a group-chat metaphor. Avoid presenting Thread internals as lifecycle management. Thread formation remains governed by existing Resonance thresholds, privacy, and safety policies.

## Product principle

> Threads reveal stories that already exist between experiences.
